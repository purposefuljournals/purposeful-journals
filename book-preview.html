CHAPTER ONE

The Man Who Knew What to Say

Danielle Hartley had stopped waiting for the phone to ring in the spring of her forty-fourth year, and she remembered that afternoon exactly.

It had not been dramatic. No slammed door, no tearful voicemail. She had been standing in the kitchen of her Georgian terrace in Bristol, peeling an orange over the sink, when she realised she had not checked her phone in three hours.

For ten minutes she stood with the peel curling in her hands and the afternoon light lying across the worktop, feeling something close to grief and something very close to relief. Then she had eaten the orange, and that was that.

Four years later, the orange was a small private joke between her and herself. The day I retired from hope, she sometimes thought.

She was forty-eight years old. She was an architect, a specialist in restoring old buildings. She had a daughter, two friends who would have driven through a storm for her, and a life she had carefully built for herself.

She was not unhappy. She was simply no longer leaving the door open.

Then came Tariq.

His message arrived on a Tuesday, in the middle of a site meeting, and she didn't see it until she was sitting in her car in the harbourside car park, the rain beating down on the roof.

He had read the thing Joy had written about her, the line about rescuing old buildings, and he had written:

I'm going to guess you're the kind of person who walks into a room and can tell within seconds where it's lying about itself. I've spent two days trying to decide whether that's a terrifying quality or the best one. I suspect it's both. — Tariq

She read it twice with the rain loud on the roof.

It had not read like a line. It read like a man standing in a doorway, saying something true a little earlier than he meant to.

◆ ◆ ◆

That evening, she wrote back from her sofa, with a glass of wine she hadn't intended to open.

Both, she wrote. "It's a terrifying quality and the best one. People don't tell you that the buildings are the easy part."

She did not expect an answer before morning. It came in four minutes.

Now I want to know what the hard part is.
